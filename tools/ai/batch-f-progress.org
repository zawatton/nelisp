** S2.12 A lambda's leading (:documentation FORM) becomes the closure's docstring slot and is never macroexpanded away: OClosure type, docstring and make-interpreted-closure, file-loaded under eager expansion and through eval, GNU golden
#+NOTE: Cause 1 of the fileio-2 failure.  The prelude defined :documentation as a no-op macro, so eager macroexpansion of a loaded file (GNU lread.c readevalloop_eager_expand_eval) erased the form from every lambda body before `function' could keep it, and every OClosure lost its type slot (advice-add failed with args-out-of-range).  GNU consumes the form in Ffunction (eval.c:594) and nothing macroexpands it.  This runtime keeps the form in the closure body, where the native slot view reads it (nl_closure_docmark_p), so the fix makes :documentation a FUNCTION: macroexpand and macroexpand-all, the runtime's and any library's, leave it alone.  Coordinator, 2026-10-22: GNU's way (Ffunction consuming the form) would need a native closure representation change; the function shim was the agreed fallback.  Per-call cost, measured on CPU 11: a closure call with a leading (:documentation 'x) takes 9.7 us under the old macro (E), 8.2 us as a function, 7.1 us without the form.  The fixture fails on e62a2be5a (aref of the file-loaded closure: args-out-of-range) and passes on E's build except for macroexpand-all, which E also got wrong.  oclosure-define and advice-add themselves need cl-preloaded, which only the library image has, so the OClosure type is tested through oclosure.el's own predicate over the slot (bfd-oclosure-type, nadvice.el's advice--p).
#+TIMEOUT: 300

** S2.13 load evaluates every form of a file under the eager-load skip marker and after it, GNU golden (a defun or defmacro with declare, then the rest of the file, twice)
#+NOTE: Cause 3 of the fileio-2 failure, found by the library owner.  With (skip) as the first element of macroexp--pending-eager-loads, internal-macroexpand-for-load answers the form unexpanded, as GNU's does, and the eager driver then evaluated a top-level macro call whose expansion the evaluator caches by the address of the driver's rooted scratch slot, the same address for every form: all later forms hit the stale row and `load' silently re-ran the first form's expansion (and every later load in the process did the same).  nl_load_eager_eval_w now evicts that row before it evaluates (nl_mxcache_evict, as bf_load_eval_loop already did for RESULT). * S5 Bytecode VM garbage-collection poll (GNU bytecode.c maybe_gc)
# Coordinator, 2026-10-22: the bytecode VM never polled for GC.  nl_gc_midform_collect ran only at the
# interpreted `while' back edge and in the native-bytecode poll; wf_bytecode_execute_frame and
# wf_bytecode_call never called it.  GNU polls at Bcall entry (bytecode.c:797) and at every 256th
# backward branch (:861-866, quitcounter).  Ported (not adapted): nl_gc_vm_poll in
# scripts/nelisp-standalone-build.el calls the existing gated collector at those two points; a local
# counter per frame is the quitcounter.  `(nelisp--debug-switch 32)' collects at every poll, 5 restores.
** S5.1 Bounded memory: 2.5 million GNU-compiled bytecode funcalls (the Bcall poll) and 30 million iterations of a call-free consing loop (the backward-branch poll) each finish under a 1 GiB peak resident set on both linkages, and batch E's build (1aa530d09) fails the same test
#+NOTE: test/standalone-vm-gc-poll-memory-check.el reads its own VmHWM after each workload.  Measured: batch E's build allocates about 0.67 KB per funcall (peak 1942 MB at 2.5 million calls; 433 MB at 300k, 837 MB at 900k) and about 65 B per loop iteration (937 MB at 10 million, 2210 to 2334 MB at 30 million); with the polls both peak at 631 MB, the idle process (about 290 MB) plus one collection window.  The loop is covered by eval/core's growth trigger (reserved chunk bytes), so gc-port's typed-block debt backstop is not needed here.  The check script runs E's build as the negative control.  The shape of the checks follows gc-port's tools/ai/gc-port-vm-poll.py leak and loop; the ceiling is 1 GiB and not their 400 MiB because eval/core's idle process is larger.
#+TIMEOUT: 600
cmd: bash tools/ai/batch-f-check.sh vm-memory
** S5.2 Every poll is GC-safe: with a full collection forced at the polls of the GNU bytecode corpus and of an allocating loop, and the conservative stack scan off, every answer still matches GNU (145/145) on both linkages, and the collections really ran
#+NOTE: test/vm-gc-poll-stress.el.  The hook is (nelisp--debug-switch 32 N): force the next N VM polls, all of them without N; (nelisp--debug-switch 28) turns the conservative native-stack scan off so that only the precise roots keep a value alive across a poll.  The criterion sets N=100 before each of the 145 cases (a recursive fib polls 1972 times and would take 144 of the 181 seconds a linkage needs for every poll of every case) and forces every poll of a 5000-iteration allocating loop; it requires at least 200 collections (nelisp--gc-diag element 7), so a build without the hook cannot pass, and the check script confirms that batch E's build fails it.  The pipeline's vm-stress-full unit runs the same file with VM_POLL_BUDGET=all, a collection at EVERY poll of every case (2459 collections), and its result is in the report.
#+TIMEOUT: 600
cmd: bash tools/ai/batch-f-check.sh vm-stress
